# S9. AgentDojo Recorded-Trajectory Audit Trail

## S9.1 Evidence type

In this supplement, counterfactual replay asks which completed consequential calls the gate would have permitted in the selected AgentDojo trajectories. For each call, the replay records the support derived from the legitimate task and the corresponding gate decision. The language model does not run again after a counterfactual denial. Thus, the recorded decision applies to the observed call and leaves the agent's subsequent behavior under denial unknown. The [coverage](#s92-coverage) section identifies the sample, and the [suite summary](#s93-suite-summary) and [action-level audit](#s94-complete-action-level-audit) give the counts and individual calls. Finally, the [findings](#s95-findings-exposed-by-action-level-analysis) and [interpretation](#s96-interpretation-boundary) sections explain the relation between native outcomes and action-level support and describe the limits of the sample.

## S9.2 Coverage

Among the 13 selected AgentDojo trajectories in the [case index](../Data_and_Code_Availability/S9_agentdojo_audit/agentdojo_stratified_case_index.json), 12 contain a completed consequential call. The sample covers 3 suites and 3 model configurations. Across Banking, Travel, and Workspace, the audit identifies 21 completed consequential calls, of which 17 lacked support and 4 had support. Under the counterfactual decisions recorded for this sample, the gate would deny 17/17 unsupported calls and permit 4/4 supported calls.

## S9.5 Findings exposed by action-level analysis

For each completed call, the audit distinguishes the authority available to execute that call from the state assessed by the native checker. A later call can change that state. When the checker observes only the resulting state, its outcome can conceal an earlier effect that lacked legitimate support. Thus, the audit assesses each recorded effect against the authority derived from the user's task for the corresponding call.

In Travel, an unsupported reservation at the Riverside View Hotel took effect before a legitimate reservation replaced it. The later reservation changed the state seen by the checker. In Workspace, the agent failed to delete the targeted file, but it cancelled a calendar event without legitimate support. In Banking, the native utility score recorded success on the user task even though the same trajectory contained unsupported calls. Thus, the distinction among the native outcomes and action-level authorization is as follows.

$$
\text{Task Utility}
\neq
\text{Attack-Goal Success}
\neq
\text{Authorization Consistency}.
$$

## S9.6 Interpretation boundary

For the 13 selected trajectories, the [replay summary](../Data_and_Code_Availability/S9_agentdojo_audit/agentdojo_stratified_replay_results.json) reports the decisions that the gate would have returned for the completed consequential calls. The selection was purposive. Since the selection depended on authorization-relevant behavior, the replay counts cannot estimate how often that behavior occurs across AgentDojo. For the earlier 3-case and 7-case pilot subsets, the records document the development of the audit and contribute no independent samples. Thus, the interpretation applies to the recorded calls in the 13 selected trajectories.
