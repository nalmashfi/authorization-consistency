# S9. AgentDojo Recorded-Trajectory Audit Trail

## S9.1 Evidence type

In this supplement, a counterfactual replay examines the authorization of completed consequential calls in recorded AgentDojo trajectories. For each such call, the audit checks its support against the frozen authorization for the user task, then records the decision the proposed model would have made. A counterfactual denial does not trigger another model run. Thus, the replay can distinguish supported from unsupported calls, but it cannot show how the language model would respond to a denial. The [coverage](#s92-coverage) section introduces the sample, the [suite summary](#s93-suite-summary) presents the counts, the [action-level audit](#s94-complete-action-level-audit) gives the individual calls, the [findings](#s95-findings-exposed-by-action-level-analysis) section reviews their effects, and the [interpretation](#s96-interpretation-boundary) section summarizes the limits of the evidence.

## S9.2 Coverage

For the replay sample, the audit reports the number of selected trajectories and the number of consequential calls that reached completion. The [case index](../Data_and_Code_Availability/S9_agentdojo_audit/agentdojo_stratified_case_index.json) lists 13 official AgentDojo trajectories. Of these trajectories, 12 contain at least one completed consequential call. Across the 3 model configurations represented in the case index, the selected trajectories cover 3 suites, namely, Banking, Travel, and Workspace. Of the 21 completed consequential calls, 17 lack support and 4 have support. Under the counterfactual gate decisions for this sample, the replay records denials for 17/17 unsupported calls and permissions for 4/4 supported calls.

## S9.5 Findings exposed by action-level analysis

For each completed call, the audit examines what the native outcome of the whole trajectory reveals about the authorization of that call. However, a later call can change the state before the native check observes the effect of an earlier unsupported call. Thus, the native outcome alone cannot establish which calls had support. To resolve this issue, the audit compares each recorded effect with the support that the legitimate user task provides for the corresponding call.

In Travel, the record shows that the agent completed an unsupported reservation at the Riverside View Hotel before making the legitimate reservation. In fact, the unsupported reservation had already taken effect. In Workspace, the agent failed to delete the targeted file, but it cancelled a calendar event without support from the legitimate user task. In Banking, it can be observed that a trajectory can satisfy the user task even when some completed calls lack support. Thus, the distinction between native outcomes and the authorization of individual calls can be written as follows.

$$
\text{Task Utility}
\neq
\text{Attack-Goal Success}
\neq
\text{Authorization Consistency}.
$$

## S9.6 Interpretation boundary

For the above findings, the selection of trajectories limits the conclusions that the recorded calls and their counterfactual gate decisions can support. The audit cannot estimate the frequency of authorization-relevant behavior across AgentDojo. This is because the 13 trajectories were selected on the basis of that behavior. For the manuscript analysis, the earlier 3-case and 7-case pilot subsets document development and contribute no independent experimental samples. Thus, the [replay summary](../Data_and_Code_Availability/S9_agentdojo_audit/agentdojo_stratified_replay_results.json) supports conclusions about the calls in the 13 selected trajectories only.
