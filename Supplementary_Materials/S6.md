# S6. Artificial Intelligence Risk-Layer Evaluation

## S6.1 Role of the risk layer

In the risk-layer evaluation, deterministic authorization determines which actions the classifier can consider for additional treatment. Only authorized actions enter this evaluation. For those actions, the risk layer uses a Gradient Boosting classifier with a configuration fixed throughout the experiment. Thus, the set of actions retained by the risk layer is a subset of the authorized actions as follows.

$$
A_t^{risk}\subseteq A_t^{auth}.
$$

## S6.2 Authorized population

For the controlled evaluation, the authorized cases fall into two groups, namely, the controlled high-risk group and the remaining authorized group. Each authorized case belongs to exactly one group. Within each group, the number of authorized cases provides the denominator for calculating the proportion of cases that receive additional treatment. For the controlled population, the table gives the total number of authorized cases and the number in each group as follows.

| Population | Authorized cases |
|---|---:|
| Total | 90,000 |
| Controlled high-risk group | 42,837 |
| Remaining authorized group | 47,163 |

## S6.3 Treatment metrics

To describe how often the classifier assigns additional treatment, each rate uses the cases in one of the two authorized groups. For the controlled high-risk group and the remaining authorized group, the measures are the Risk Treatment Rate (RTR) and the Friction Introduction Rate (FIR), respectively. Only cases receiving additional treatment enter the numerator. For each rate, the denominator includes all authorized cases in the corresponding group, giving the two proportions as follows.

$$
\mathrm{RTR}=
\frac{N_{\text{high-risk authorized cases receiving treatment}}}
{N_{\text{high-risk authorized cases}}},
$$

$$
\mathrm{FIR}=
\frac{N_{\text{remaining authorized cases receiving treatment}}}
{N_{\text{remaining authorized cases}}}.
$$

## S6.5 Scope of the evidence

Within the authorized population, RTR and FIR measure the frequency of additional treatment that the classifier assigns to each group. Neither measure gives the accuracy of deterministic authorization. If an action fails deterministic authorization, the classifier cannot grant the authority the action requires for execution. For the evaluated classifier, the records give the observed treatment rates, but omit the exact hyperparameters and the risk predictions for individual traces. Thus, the available records do not support an exact rerun of the classifier.
