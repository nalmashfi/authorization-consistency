# S5. Stochastic-Model Validation

## S5.1 State model

From an existing authorization inconsistency, the process can pass through transient states that describe its progression toward an external effect. An absorbing state ends the progression. At absorption, the process reaches one of two outcomes, namely, containment of the inconsistency or a harmful external effect. For each transient or absorbing state in the model, the corresponding symbol and the condition that it represents are as follows.

| State class | Symbol | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Transient | $\mathsf{I}$ | An authorization inconsistency exists. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{P}$ | The authorization inconsistency persists. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{R}$ | The authorization inconsistency resurfaces. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{D}$ | The authorization inconsistency propagates through delegation. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{A}$ | An unauthorized consequential action is attempted. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{G}$ | The process reaches an unauthorized protected-commit state. |
| Absorbing | $\mathsf{C}$ | The process reaches containment. |
| Absorbing | $\mathsf{H}$ | A harmful external effect occurs. |

## S5.2 Sequential model

For the sequential path, the process reaches a harmful external effect only if it completes every transition leading to that effect. Each transition has a conditional probability. To evaluate a transition, the model conditions its probability on the process having reached the state from which that transition starts. Thus, multiplication of the conditional probabilities along the path gives the probability of a harmful external effect as follows.

$$
P_H=q_Pq_Rq_Dq_Aq_Gq_H.
$$

## S5.3 General absorbing chain

In the general absorbing chain, the transition matrix separates transitions among transient states from transitions into absorbing states. Once the process reaches an absorbing state, it remains there. For a process starting in any transient state, the block of transitions among transient states determines the fundamental matrix. To compute the absorption probabilities for each transient starting state, the model multiplies the fundamental matrix by the block of transitions into absorbing states. For this calculation, the transition matrix, fundamental matrix, and matrix of absorption probabilities are as follows.

$$
\mathbf P=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf Q & \mathbf R\\
0 & \mathbf I
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\mathbf N=(\mathbf I-\mathbf Q)^{-1},
\qquad
\mathbf B=\mathbf N\mathbf R.
$$

## S5.4 Validation metrics

Within each setting, the empirical frequency of a harmful external effect provides the reference for the probability predicted by the stochastic model. Across settings, mean absolute error (MAE) measures the average magnitude of the differences between predicted and empirical probabilities. By squaring each difference before averaging, root mean squared error (RMSE) gives greater weight to larger differences. For each formulation, correlation measures how the paired probabilities vary together across the settings examined in the validation. For the paired probabilities, the two error measures are defined as follows.

$$
\mathrm{MAE}=
\frac1n\sum_i
\left|\widehat P_{H,i}-P_{H,i}^{emp}\right|,
$$

$$
\mathrm{RMSE}=
\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_i
\left(\widehat P_{H,i}-P_{H,i}^{emp}\right)^2}.
$$

## S5.6 Scope of the evidence

Given an existing authorization inconsistency, each stochastic model estimates the probability that the inconsistency progresses to a harmful external effect. Deterministic authorization governs whether an individual action can execute. For both formulations, the release provides the aggregate measures and the final figure, but omits the predicted and empirical probabilities for the individual settings. Thus, the reported values support a comparison of the two formulations, but independent recalculation of the measures requires the underlying experiment records.
