# S5. Stochastic-Model Validation

## S5.1 State model

In the state model, each transient state identifies a stage that an authorization inconsistency can reach as it progresses toward an outcome. An absorbing state marks the end of that progression. At absorption, the process reaches one of two outcomes, namely, containment of the inconsistency or a harmful external effect. For each state, the table gives its class, its symbol, and the condition represented by that symbol as follows.

| State class | Symbol | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Transient | $\mathsf{I}$ | An authorization inconsistency exists. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{P}$ | The authorization inconsistency persists. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{R}$ | The authorization inconsistency resurfaces. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{D}$ | The authorization inconsistency propagates through delegation. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{A}$ | An unauthorized consequential action is attempted. |
| Transient | $\mathsf{G}$ | The process reaches an unauthorized protected-commit state. |
| Absorbing | $\mathsf{C}$ | The process reaches containment. |
| Absorbing | $\mathsf{H}$ | A harmful external effect occurs. |

## S5.2 Sequential model

In the sequential model, the probability of a harmful external effect depends on each transition along the specified path. This is because the process can reach the harmful external effect along that path only if every preceding transition occurs. For each transition along the path, the probability is conditional on the process having reached the preceding state. Thus, the product of the conditional transition probabilities gives the probability of a harmful external effect along the path as follows.

$$
P_H=q_Pq_Rq_Dq_Aq_Gq_H.
$$

## S5.3 General absorbing chain

For the general absorbing chain, the transition matrix groups the transition probabilities according to the source and destination states. Once the process enters an absorbing state, it remains there. The transient-to-transient block determines the fundamental matrix. Multiplying the fundamental matrix by the transient-to-absorbing block then gives the probability of reaching each absorbing state from each transient state. For the general absorbing chain, the transition matrix and the two matrices derived from its blocks are as follows.

$$
\mathbf P=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf Q & \mathbf R\\
0 & \mathbf I
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\mathbf N=(\mathbf I-\mathbf Q)^{-1},
\qquad
\mathbf B=\mathbf N\mathbf R.
$$

## S5.4 Validation metrics

To assess agreement for each stochastic model, the validation compares the estimated probability of a harmful external effect with the empirical probability for each setting. Across the evaluated settings, two measures summarize the error, namely, mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE). Correlation measures how the two sets of probabilities vary together. For the paired estimated and empirical probabilities, the expressions for the two error measures are as follows.

$$
\mathrm{MAE}=
\frac1n\sum_i
\left|\widehat P_{H,i}-P_{H,i}^{emp}\right|,
$$

$$
\mathrm{RMSE}=
\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_i
\left(\widehat P_{H,i}-P_{H,i}^{emp}\right)^2}.
$$

## S5.6 Scope of the evidence

For the evaluated settings, the stochastic models describe the progression of an authorization inconsistency toward a harmful external effect. The deterministic authorization model determines whether an action can execute. For each stochastic model, the repository contains the summary statistics and the final figure, but omits the trace table for individual settings. Thus, the reported measures permit comparison across models, while recomputing those measures for each model requires the underlying experiment records.
